●	Your first 5 patients
●	Your first 25 patients
●	Role-specific training for every team member
●	Scripted language so no one has to guess what to say
●	Pricing strategies that actually work in 2026
●	Documentation that protects you
●	A 90-day roadmap so you know exactly what to do each week

VIDEO #3: "The Hygienist Discovery System" (12 min)
Setting: Operatory with hygienist and patient actor
 Audience: Hygienists only
 Source: Bullet #3.1
________________________________________
SCRIPT
[OPEN: Hygienist seated with patient in chair, camera on hygienist]
[0:00-1:00] - INTRODUCTION
[Hygienist to camera]
"If you're a hygienist, you already know something powerful: patients tell you things they don't tell the dentist.
They mention jaw pain. They complain about wrinkles. They point to receding gums and ask, 'Is this getting worse?'
But most hygienists don't know what to do with that information. So it dies in the chair.
This system changes that. You're going to learn how to identify opportunities, advocate for your patients, and tee up care - without ever feeling like you're selling."
[1:00-2:30] - THE UNIVERSAL PERMISSION QUESTION
[Hygienist turns to patient actor]
"Let me show you the most important question you'll ever ask.
It happens once per visit, near the end of the hygiene appointment, before the doctor comes in.
You ask this:
'Before Dr. [Name] comes in, is there anything about your teeth, your smile, or facial comfort or appearance that you'd like me to make sure they address today?'
That's it. Neutral tone. You ask once. You don't probe. You don't follow up."
[Patient actor responds: "No, I'm good."]
[Hygienist to patient]
"Okay."
[Hygienist to camera]
"And you move on. You don't revisit it. This preserves trust.
But when they say yes..."
[Patient actor: "Actually, yeah - I've been thinking about Botox. Do you do that here?"]
[Hygienist to patient]
"Absolutely - I'll make sure Dr. [Name] knows."
[Hygienist to camera]
"And then you stop talking about it. The dentist takes over. That's the handoff."
[2:30-4:30] - PATIENT PERMISSION SIGNALS
[Hygienist to camera]
"Now, sometimes patients give you a signal even if you don't ask the question.
Here's what signals look like:"
[ROLE-PLAY MONTAGE - 4 quick scenarios]
Scenario 1:
●	Patient: "Ugh, I hate these wrinkles."
●	Hygienist: "You're not alone - a lot of patients mention that. That's something we address here now - Dr. [Name] can explain what makes sense for you."
Scenario 2:
●	Patient: "My jaw has been killing me lately."
●	Hygienist: "That's a very common concern. That's definitely something Dr. [Name] evaluates with patients."
Scenario 3:
●	Patient: "My gums look like they're shrinking."
●	Hygienist: "We hear that quite a bit. That's something Dr. [Name] can walk you through options for."
Scenario 4:
●	Patient: "Do you guys do Botox here?"
●	Hygienist: "We do. Dr. [Name] can talk through whether it makes sense for you."
[Back to hygienist addressing camera]
"Notice the pattern?
Step 1: Reflect and normalize - 'You're not alone,' 'That's common,' 'We hear that.'
Step 2: Bridge to the dentist - 'Dr. [Name] can explain,' 'Dr. [Name] evaluates that.'
Step 3: Stop talking. Do not explain. Do not elaborate. Do not answer follow-up questions.
If they ask more, you say: 'That's a great question for Dr. [Name] - they'll explain it better than I can.'"
[4:30-7:00] - CLINICAL DISCOVERY (NON-ESTHETIC)
[Hygienist to camera]
"Now, even if the patient doesn't ask about anything, you still need to look for clinical findings.
You're looking for three categories:"
[ON SCREEN: Graphics of each category]
"Category 1: Gum and Tissue Issues
●	Recession
●	Black triangles
●	Root sensitivity
●	Thin tissue
Category 2: Clenching and Bruxism Indicators
●	Wear facets
●	Scalloped tongue
●	Linea alba
●	Patient mentions headaches or jaw soreness
Category 3: 'Watch' Conditions
●	Previously documented recession that's progressing
●	Chronic sensitivity with no clear cause
●	Areas you've been monitoring"
[Hygienist demonstrates with patient actor]
[Hygienist examining patient's mouth]
"I'm noticing some recession starting here."
[Hygienist to camera]
"That's all you say. You've documented the finding. Now you use the tee-up."
[7:00-9:00] - THE CLINICAL TEE-UPS (MEMORIZE THESE)
[Hygienist to camera]
"For each clinical finding, you have one approved tee-up line. Memorize these."
[ROLE-PLAY: Hygienist with patient]
For Gum Recession:
"We have a non-surgical way to stabilize and rebuild gum tissue now - I'll have Dr. [Name] explain it."
For TMJ/Clenching:
"We're seeing really good results treating this beyond appliances alone - Dr. [Name] will explain."
For Watch Conditions:
"This is something we don't just watch anymore - Dr. [Name] will go over options."
[Hygienist to camera]
"Short. Clinical. Confident. And then you hand off."
[9:00-10:30] - WHAT YOU MUST NEVER SAY
[Hygienist to camera, serious tone]
"There are phrases that will kill trust and conversion. Never say these:
❌ 'It's cosmetic'
 ❌ 'It's optional'
 ❌ 'It's expensive'
 ❌ 'I don't know much about it'
 ❌ 'You can think about it'
 ❌ 'They're just trying this'
 ❌ 'It's new for us'
If you're unsure what to say... say nothing and escalate to the dentist."
[10:30-11:45] - THE HANDOFF
[Hygienist to camera]
"After you identify something, here's what you do:
Step 1: Chart the finding or patient concern word-for-word.
Step 2: Brief the dentist verbally before they enter. Tell them what you observed and what the patient said.
Step 3: Exit the conversation or stay silent.
The dentist now owns the recommendation. Your job is done."
[11:45-12:00] - CLOSE
[Hygienist to camera]
"This system works because you're not selling. You're listening, identifying, and advocating.
Patients will feel seen, not judged. And doctors will have clean handoffs to make confident recommendations.
That's how injectables succeed in dentistry."

VIDEO #4: "Dentist Authority Language" (15 min)
Setting: Operatory with dentist, patient actor, assistant
 Audience: Dentist only
 Source: Bullet #3.4
________________________________________
SCRIPT
[OPEN: Dentist direct-to-camera in operatory]
[0:00-1:30] - THE AUTHORITY PROBLEM
"Let's talk about why you're hesitating to recommend injectable care.
It's not because you lack skill. You've been trained. You know the anatomy, the dosing, the technique.
You're hesitating because you feel like you're selling.
And the moment you feel like a salesperson, your language softens:
●	'If you want...'
●	'You could consider...'
●	'It's up to you...'
And when you soften, patients sense uncertainty. And uncertain patients don't say yes.
This system fixes that. You're going to learn how to recommend care with calm authority - the same way you recommend a crown or a nightguard."
[1:30-3:30] - THE AUTHORITY FRAME
[Dentist to camera]
"Your opening sets the tone. Here's how you enter the conversation after a hygiene tee-up or patient signal."
[ROLE-PLAY: Dentist enters operatory where hygienist has teed up gum recession concern]
[Dentist to patient]
"So Sarah mentioned you've been noticing some recession here. Let me take a look."
[Dentist examines]
"Based on what I'm seeing, here's what I'd recommend..."
[Back to camera]
"Notice the language:
●	'Based on what I'm seeing' - diagnostic frame
●	'Here's what I'd recommend' - declarative, not asking permission
You're not saying: ❌ 'If you want...'
 ❌ 'You could think about...'
 ❌ 'Have you ever considered...'
You're diagnosing and recommending. That's your job."
[3:30-6:00] - THE CLINICAL EXPLANATION (SHORT & GROUNDED)
[Dentist to camera]
"Your explanation should answer three questions only:
1.	What's happening
2.	Why it matters
3.	What can be done
Let me show you:"
[ROLE-PLAY: Three scenarios]
Scenario 1: Gum Builder (Hygiene Model)
[Dentist to patient]
"What's happening is your gum tissue is thinning and pulling back from the root surface. Over time, that leads to more sensitivity and potential bone loss. The reason I'm recommending Gum Builder is it allows us to stabilize and thicken this tissue without surgery."
Scenario 2: TMJ Botox
[Dentist to patient]
"Your masseter muscles are overactive from chronic clenching. That's causing the jaw pain and headaches you're experiencing, and it's also creating excessive force on your teeth - which is why we're seeing these fractures. Therapeutic Botox reduces that muscle hyperactivity, which gives you relief and protects your dentistry."
Scenario 3: Esthetic Botox
[Dentist to patient]
"The lines you're noticing in your forehead are from repetitive muscle movement over time. Botox relaxes those muscles temporarily, which softens the lines. It's very routine, and the results typically last 3-4 months."
[Back to camera]
"See the pattern? Clinical. Calm. Factual. No hype. No overselling.
Then you stop. Do not lecture. Do not list every possible option. Give them what they need to decide."
[6:00-8:30] - THE RECOMMENDATION (WHERE MOST DENTISTS FAIL)
[Dentist to camera, serious]
"This is where most dentists weaken. They get to the recommendation and suddenly start asking permission:
❌ 'Would you like to try this?'
 ❌ 'What do you think?'
 ❌ 'Does that sound interesting?'
Stop. You are the clinician. Use declarative language:"
[ROLE-PLAY montage of correct recommendations]
"My recommendation is to start with Gum Builder for these areas."
"The next step I'd take is therapeutic Botox to reduce the muscle activity."
"I'd start with treating the glabella and forehead, and we'll reassess in 3 months."
[Back to camera]
"Notice: You're not asking. You're recommending.
Patients still decide. But you must lead.
The moment you say 'It's up to you' or 'If you want,' you've abdicated authority and they'll feel uncertain."
[8:30-10:30] - EXPECTATION SETTING (PREVENTS REGRET)
[Dentist to camera]
"Now, after you recommend, you must set expectations. This prevents buyer's remorse and callbacks."
[ROLE-PLAY: Dentist with patient after recommending Botox]
[Dentist to patient]
"A couple things to expect: You'll start noticing softening around day 3-5, with full effect around day 10-14. The goal here is improvement, not perfection - we're looking for a natural result. And this typically lasts 3-4 months, so we'll want to reassess and plan your next cycle."
[Another scenario: Gum Builder]
[Dentist to patient]
"This is gradual, not dramatic. What we're aiming for is stabilization and tissue thickening over the next 4-6 weeks. Most patients notice sensitivity improvement fairly quickly. We'll reassess at your follow-up and determine if a second treatment makes sense."
[Back to camera]
"You're managing expectations up front. No guarantees. No hype. Just honesty."
[10:30-12:30] - HANDLING COMMON OBJECTIONS
[Dentist to camera]
"Let's handle the three most common responses:"
[ROLE-PLAY: Three objection scenarios]
Objection 1: "I need to think about it."
[Dentist to patient, calm]
"That's reasonable. From a clinical standpoint, this is the direction I'd recommend. Let's either schedule it or set a time to revisit."
[Back to camera]
"You reaffirm the recommendation without creating urgency. The clinical frame holds."
________________________________________
Objection 2: "Is this cosmetic?"
[Dentist to patient]
"In this case, it's about function and prevention. Appearance improvement is a side benefit."
[Back to camera]
"Reframe. Then stop."
________________________________________
Objection 3: "I've never heard of this."
[Dentist to patient]
"That's common - dentistry hasn't had a good solution for this until recently."
[Back to camera]
"Normalize. Then stop. Don't over-explain."
[12:30-14:00] - THE HANDOFF
[Dentist to camera]
"Once you've made your recommendation, you hand off to your team. Watch this:"
[ROLE-PLAY: Dentist completes recommendation]
[Dentist to patient]
"So my recommendation is Gum Builder for these three teeth. Let's get that on the schedule."
[Dentist turns to assistant]
"Can you help [Patient] get that on the schedule?"
[Back to camera]
"See what happened? You stopped talking. You signaled certainty. The team takes over.
If you keep talking, you create space for doubt. Hand off cleanly."
[14:00-15:00] - CLOSE
[Dentist to camera]
"Your authority is not forceful. It's calm, confident, and directional.
Patients want to be guided. This system lets you do that without pressure or discomfort.
Practice these phrases. Rehearse with your team. And watch what happens when you stop asking permission and start recommending care."
[FADE OUT: "Next: Watch '90-Day Integration Roadmap'"]

### PART 1: YOUR JOB (1:00-2:30)

**[SLIDE: "Assistant Role = Normalization"]**

**INSTRUCTOR:**

"Your job is NOT to sell injectables.
Your job is NOT to explain benefits.
Your job is NOT to answer clinical questions.

Your job is **normalization**.

What does that mean?

Normalization = making the patient feel like this is completely ordinary for this practice.

**Three ways you normalize:**

1. **Language** — How you talk about it
2. **Preparation** — How you set up
3. **Aftercare** — What you say after treatment

Let's break down each one."

---

### PART 2: NORMALIZATION LANGUAGE (2:30-4:30)

**[SLIDE: "What You Say vs. What You Don't Say"]**

**INSTRUCTOR:**

"Here's what kills normalization:

❌ 'So you're getting Botox today — exciting!'  
❌ 'Have you done this before?'  
❌ 'Don't worry, it won't hurt much.'  
❌ 'Dr. [Name] is getting really good at this.'

Why are these bad?

Because they frame injectables as:
- Special (exciting)
- Uncertain (asking if first time)
- Scary (mentioning pain)
- New for the practice (still learning)

**Here's what normalizes:**

✅ 'We'll start with your injectable treatment, then finish with your cleaning.'  
✅ 'Let me get the ice pack and consent ready.'  
✅ 'You'll feel a small pinch — less than anesthetic.'  
✅ 'Dr. [Name] will walk you through what to expect.'

Notice the difference?

Injectable = **just another part of the appointment**.

Not special. Not scary. Just routine."

---

**[SLIDE: "Chairside Language Examples"]**

**INSTRUCTOR:**

"Here are your four go-to phrases. Memorize these:

**1. TREATMENT SETUP**  
'I'll get everything ready for your injectable treatment.'

**2. DURING TREATMENT**  
'You're doing great. Almost done.'

**3. AFTERCARE HANDOFF**  
'Here's what to expect over the next few hours. Any bruising should be minimal.'

**4. CHECKOUT**  
'We'll want to see you back in about 3-4 months for your next cycle. Let's get that scheduled.'

That's it. Four phrases.

Don't overcomplicate it. Don't add commentary. Just execute."

---

### PART 3: PREPARATION (4:30-6:00)

**[SLIDE: "Setup Protocol"]**

**INSTRUCTOR:**

"How you prepare the treatment area matters.

If you're scrambling to find supplies, the patient notices.  
If you're calm and organized, the patient relaxes.

**Your prep checklist (before patient enters):**

1. ✅ Consent form on clipboard (ready to sign)
2. ✅ Ice pack or topical anesthetic (if used)
3. ✅ Treatment tray set up (vials, syringes, gauze)
4. ✅ Mirror ready (for patient to see areas)
5. ✅ Aftercare sheet printed (to hand them on exit)

**During treatment:**

- Hand items to doctor without being asked
- Anticipate next step (gauze, cotton, mirror)
- Stay calm if patient flinches or asks questions
- Redirect clinical questions to dentist: 'Dr. [Name] can explain that.'

**After treatment:**

- Clean up while dentist gives aftercare instructions
- Hand patient the aftercare sheet
- Walk them to checkout: 'Front desk will get your next visit scheduled.'

This flow should feel **choreographed**, not chaotic."

---
PART 4: AFTERCARE DELIVERY (6:00-7:30)
**[SLIDE: "Post-Treatment Instructions"]**
**INSTRUCTOR:**

"After treatment, you hand them the aftercare sheet and say:

'Here's what to expect over the next few hours. Any bruising should be minimal. You'll start noticing results in 3-5 days.'

Key points they need to know:
- ✅ Avoid rubbing/massaging area for 4 hours
- ✅ Avoid lying down for 4 hours (Botox only)
- ✅ Minor bruising or redness is normal
- ✅ Results appear gradually (3-5 days for Botox, 2 weeks for fillers)
- ✅ Call if any concerns
Then: 'Any questions?'
If they have questions → redirect to the dentist if clinical.  
If they ask about booking the next visit → 'Front desk will help you schedule.'
Your role = information handoff, not counseling session."

---
PART 5: WHAT NOT TO DO (7:30-8:30)
**[SLIDE: "Common Mistakes"]**
**INSTRUCTOR:**
"Avoid these assistant traps:

❌ **Over-enthusiastic commentary**  
'You're going to look amazing!' — Makes it about vanity, not health.

❌ **Apologizing for the practice**  
'Sorry we're still learning this.' — Kills trust.

❌ **Sharing personal stories**  
'I've always wanted to try Botox!' — Makes it about you, not them.

❌ **Answering clinical questions**  
'How many units will I need?' — That's the dentist's job.

❌ **Creating urgency**  
'Book now before we run out!' — Sounds desperate.

**Just stay neutral. Calm. Professional. Routine.**

When injectables feel boring to you, they feel safe to the patient."

### [SCENE 2: PART 1 - Neurotoxins Explained]

**[1:00-6:00]**

**[Instructor to camera]**

**[GRAPHIC: Neurotoxin molecule animation]**

"**PART 1: NEUROTOXINS**

**What they are:**
Neurotoxins are injectable medications that temporarily relax muscles.

**[SHOW: Product vials - Xeomin, Botox, Daxxify]**

Our practice uses [AAFE-approved products]:
- **Xeomin** - 'Naked' neurotoxin, no additives

**[ANIMATION: Muscle contraction → neurotoxin blocking signal → muscle relaxation]**

## PART 4: RED FLAG QUESTIONS (1 MINUTE)

### [8:30-9:30] - WHEN TO ALERT THE DENTIST

**[Office Manager to camera]**

"Some questions should raise a red flag and require dentist involvement:

**RED FLAG 1: Unrealistic Expectations**  
Patient: 'I want to look like [celebrity]' or 'Can you make me look 20 years younger?'  
→ Politely acknowledge, redirect to dentist for realistic expectation setting

**RED FLAG 2: Body Dysmorphia Signals**  
Patient: 'I've had this done 10 times and it never looks right' or obsessive focus on minor perceived flaws  
→ Alert dentist privately before consultation

**RED FLAG 3: Price Shopping with Concerning Language**  
Patient: 'I can get this cheaper at [medspa], why are you so expensive?' or 'Just give me a discount'  
→ Don't engage. Redirect: 'Our pricing reflects Dr. [Name]'s training and our safety standards. I'm happy to schedule a consultation to discuss your goals.'

**RED FLAG 4: Pressure for Immediate Treatment**  
Patient: 'I need this done today, I have an event tonight'  
→ Don't rush. Follow consultation protocol.

**RED FLAG 5: Medical History Concerns**  
Patient mentions: pregnancy, breastfeeding, neuromuscular conditions, blood thinners, recent surgeries  
→ Immediately defer to dentist: 'Let me have Dr. [Name] review your medical history before we schedule.'"
